# Majlis design notes (second redesign)

## What good party trivia games do
- **Seen Jeem (سين جيم):** the closest relative. The board is a wall of category cards, each led by a big picture, with plain white point tiles you can read from the sofa. Two teams and their helper abilities sit in a strip at the bottom. The pictures carry the board; there is almost no chrome.
- **Kahoot:** saturated colour blocks, very large type, one action per screen, and colour plus shape for every answer. It reads from across a room on a projector.
- **Jackbox:** each game has one strong art direction with illustrated characters and props. The UI stays out of the way and the art and copy supply the personality. Motion is saved for reveals.
- **HQ Trivia:** one question, one big clock, and a clear reveal moment. Nothing else competes on screen.

## Principles applied to Majlis
1. **Pictures do the visual work.** Every category has a 3D illustration (Microsoft Fluent Emoji, MIT licence) shown large on its board card, in the category picker, on the question screen and on the home screen. Teams get animal mascots.
2. **Readable from the sofa.** Point tiles are white with big dark numbers, and question text is large. Category names sit on solid colour.
3. **Colour means something.** Each category group has one vivid colour, which fills its cards. Team colours are only used for team state (turn, scores, won tiles). One sun-yellow is the main action colour.
4. **Less decoration.** No woven bands, dashed outlines or icon boxes. Surfaces are flat colour with one pressed edge on things you tap.
5. **One moment of motion.** The home screen art floats in, and answers pop on reveal. Everything else stays still.
6. **Playful type that also covers Arabic.** Baloo Bhaijaan 2 for headings and numbers, Rubik for text. Both include Arabic, so "English - Arabic" lines match.
